# Drift Loom

Drift Loom treats the glyph field as evidence of a living language moving through a larger atmosphere. Individual marks are not decorative text and do not behave as a screen saver. They drift slowly through bounded, seeded fields, leaving enough empty space for people, photographs, and meaning to remain primary.

The field uses a small value-noise current combined with sine modulation. That keeps movement organic without the expense or visual chaos of a large flocking simulation. Every glyph has a unique life duration, phase, anchor, and micro-size, so a new generation develops naturally while its entries and exits stay imperceptible.

Cursor proximity is a reveal rather than an avoidance response. Nearby glyphs gain a little luminance and follow a tangential force around the pointer, like dust caught in a slow current. A procedural brightness threshold removes parts of the ambient field until touch or darkness makes them legible, producing a restrained dither texture without reading or sampling the page beneath it.

At chapter thresholds, only a sparse third of the field settles toward invisible sampled points from a Karen numeral. The target is a translucent constellation, never a stamped block of text. The symbol breathes through one global coordinate scale, then relaxes into the same drift field with an ease-out-quint departure. The implementation caps DPR, monitors frame time, lowers active count when frames are slow, pauses offscreen, and never uses DOM particle nodes.

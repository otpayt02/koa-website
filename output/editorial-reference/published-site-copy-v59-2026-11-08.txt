PAGE /en/about
Rooted in community, built for the long term.
The Karen Organization of America exists to support Karen families in the U.S. and to carry the Karen community's voice into national policy regarding Myanmar (Burma).
From the KOA photo archive · Conference gathering
Our mission
Standing with the Karen community in America, here and along the border.
Preserving Karen language across generations, promoting appreciation for cultural identity, and providing a voice for those in Kawthoolei and Burma through advocacy in Washington.
From the KOA photo archive · Outdoor gathering
What we do
KOA's work spans direct support for newly resettled families, civic and cultural programming that keeps Karen identity strong, and policy advocacy that carries Karen voices into U.S. government decisions on Myanmar and Burma.
Education Program
Helping Karen families navigate life in a new country, from early arrival needs to longer-term community connection.
Civic engagement & culture
Karen New Year celebrations, Karen literacy and language resources, and civic programs that pass identity and participation on to the next generation.
Policy advocacy
Engaging Congress and federal agencies on legislation and policy affecting Myanmar and Burma and the Karen people, including the BRAVE Burma Act.
Education training
Guidance for college readiness, academic resources, job applications and interview preparation, citizenship testing, lawful awareness, and English literacy and language resources.
Specific partners, schedules, and referrals are being confirmed.
A voice in the room
“The Karen community's story doesn't end at resettlement — it continues in the decisions Washington makes about Myanmar and Burma.”
— Karen Organization of America
Our approach
We work at three levels at once: direct, practical support for individuals and families; community and cultural programming that keeps identity strong; and policy engagement in Washington, D.C. that connects community experience to legislation and federal decision-making.
Our values
Community first
Programs and advocacy priorities are shaped by what Karen families and community members actually need.
Cultural continuity
Language, tradition, and history are treated as living things worth actively passing on, not just preserving.
Principled advocacy
We engage government processes directly and transparently, on behalf of the community we represent.
Leadership and history
Leadership biographies and confirmed milestones are being prepared for this page.

Five chapters
A story told in song.
၁
The sound of home
Karen hymns carry the memory of villages, refugee camps, and church gatherings across three continents. The music director program preserves these songs in durable digital form.
၂
What we preserve
Handwritten chord charts, church hymnals, oral arrangements, and community recordings — each one catalogued, rights-checked, and archived with provenance.
၃
What we build
A bilingual chord-chart editor with Karen typing mode, print-ready PDF export, and a chord-chart library of 21+ Karen music pieces. Built for Karen music directors in America.
ၔ
Who leads the music
Karen music directors lead worship, teach young singers, and arrange hymns for community gatherings. KOA equips them with tools and connects them to each other.
၅
How to contribute
Upload a handwritten chart, submit a recording, or suggest a lyric correction. Every contribution passes through a rights review before publication.
The editor
A bilingual tool for Karen music directors.
The chord-chart editor supports Karen typing mode, chord placement above lyric lines, and print-ready PDF export. Use it to preserve existing hymns or compose new arrangements.
01
Bilingual editor
Write lyrics in S'gaw Karen and English side by side. Karen typing mode maps your keyboard to Myanmar/Karen script.
Implemented — Karen + English, reviewed
02
Print-ready sheet music
Export chord charts as PDFs with Karen-script lyrics, chord symbols, and proper hymn formatting. Two systems per page for hymns.
Implemented — PDF export with Karen script
03
Chord-chart library
21+ Karen music chart files including hymns, youth songs, and contemporary pieces. Hand-scanned charts from community elders.
Declared — library grows with each contribution
04
Karen music generation
Melody templates using Karen-scale grammar. Generative music in Karen styles is planned but requires dataset provenance first.
In development — melody templates only; no generative model yet

Karen music style
Meter, melody, and meaning.
Karen hymns follow established patterns — predominantly 4/4 meter, verse–chorus form, I–IV–V–I harmony with stepwise melody within an octave. Lyrics use S'gaw Karen script with English chord symbols above the line.

The capability exists and is backed by a specific implementation, but has not been independently measured on a held-out test set.
Measured

Rooted in community, built for the long term.
The Karen Organization of America exists to support Karen families in the U.S. and to carry the Karen community's voice into national policy regarding Myanmar (Burma).
From the KOA photo archive · Conference gathering
Our mission
Standing with the Karen community in America, here and along the border.
Preserving Karen language across generations, promoting appreciation for cultural identity, and providing a voice for those in Kawthoolei and Burma through advocacy in Washington.
From the KOA photo archive · Outdoor gathering
What we do
KOA's work spans direct support for newly resettled families, civic and cultural programming that keeps Karen identity strong, and policy advocacy that carries Karen voices into U.S. government decisions on Myanmar and Burma.
Education Program
Helping Karen families navigate life in a new country, from early arrival needs to longer-term community connection.
Civic engagement & culture
Karen New Year celebrations, Karen literacy and language resources, and civic programs that pass identity and participation on to the next generation.
Policy advocacy
Engaging Congress and federal agencies on legislation and policy affecting Myanmar and Burma and the Karen people, including the BRAVE Burma Act.
Education training
Guidance for college readiness, academic resources, job applications and interview preparation, citizenship testing, lawful awareness, and English literacy and language resources.
Specific partners, schedules, and referrals are being confirmed.
A voice in the room
“The Karen community's story doesn't end at resettlement — it continues in the decisions Washington makes about Myanmar and Burma.”
— Karen Organization of America
Our approach
We work at three levels at once: direct, practical support for individuals and families; community and cultural programming that keeps identity strong; and policy engagement in Washington, D.C. that connects community experience to legislation and federal decision-making.
Our values
Community first
Programs and advocacy priorities are shaped by what Karen families and community members actually need.
Cultural continuity
Language, tradition, and history are treated as living things worth actively passing on, not just preserving.
Principled advocacy
We engage government processes directly and transparently, on behalf of the community we represent.

Five chapters
A story told in song.
၁
The sound of home
Karen hymns carry the memory of villages, refugee camps, and church gatherings across three continents. The music director program preserves these songs in durable digital form.
၂
What we preserve
Handwritten chord charts, church hymnals, oral arrangements, and community recordings — each one catalogued, rights-checked, and archived with provenance.
၃
What we build
A bilingual chord-chart editor with Karen typing mode, print-ready PDF export, and a chord-chart library of 21+ Karen music pieces. Built for Karen music directors in America.
ၔ
Who leads the music
Karen music directors lead worship, teach young singers, and arrange hymns for community gatherings. KOA equips them with tools and connects them to each other.
၅
How to contribute
Upload a handwritten chart, submit a recording, or suggest a lyric correction. Every contribution passes through a rights review before publication.
The editor
A bilingual tool for Karen music directors.
The chord-chart editor supports Karen typing mode, chord placement above lyric lines, and print-ready PDF export. Use it to preserve existing hymns or compose new arrangements.

Rooted in community, built for the long term.
The Karen Organization of America exists to support Karen families in the U.S. and to carry the Karen community's voice into national policy regarding Myanmar (Burma).
From the KOA photo archive · Conference gathering
Our mission
Standing with the Karen community in America, here and along the border.
Preserving Karen language across generations, promoting appreciation for cultural identity, and providing a voice for those in Kawthoolei and Burma through advocacy in Washington.
From the KOA photo archive · Outdoor gathering
What we do
KOA's work spans direct support for newly resettled families, civic and cultural programming that keeps Karen identity strong, and policy advocacy that carries Karen voices into U.S. government decisions on Myanmar and Burma.
Education Program
Helping Karen families navigate life in a new country, from early arrival needs to longer-term community connection.
Civic engagement & culture
Karen New Year celebrations, Karen literacy and language resources, and civic programs that pass identity and participation on to the next generation.
Policy advocacy
Engaging Congress and federal agencies on legislation and policy affecting Myanmar and Burma and the Karen people, including the BRAVE Burma Act.
Education training
Guidance for college readiness, academic resources, job applications and interview preparation, citizenship testing, lawful awareness, and English literacy and language resources.
Specific partners, schedules, and referrals are being confirmed.
A voice in the room
“The Karen community's story doesn't end at resettlement — it continues in the decisions Washington makes about Myanmar and Burma.”
— Karen Organization of America
Our approach
We work at three levels at once: direct, practical support for individuals and families; community and cultural programming that keeps identity strong; and policy engagement in Washington, D.C. that connects community experience to legislation and federal decision-making.
Our values
Community first
Programs and advocacy priorities are shaped by what Karen families and community members actually need.
Cultural continuity
Language, tradition, and history are treated as living things worth actively passing on, not just preserving.
Principled advocacy
We engage government processes directly and transparently, on behalf of the community we represent.

Five chapters

၁
The sound of home
Karen hymns carry the memory of villages, refugee camps, and church gatherings across three continents. The music director program preserves these songs in durable digital form.
၂
What we preserve
Handwritten chord charts, church hymnals, oral arrangements, and community recordings — each one catalogued, rights-checked, and archived with provenance.
၃
What we build
A bilingual chord-chart editor with Karen typing mode, print-ready PDF export, and a chord-chart library of 21+ Karen music pieces. Built for Karen music directors in America.
ၔ
Who leads the music
Karen music directors lead worship, teach young singers, and arrange hymns for community gatherings. KOA equips them with tools and connects them to each other.
၅
How to contribute
Upload a handwritten chart, submit a recording, or suggest a lyric correction. Every contribution passes through a rights review before publication.

Karen music style
Meter, melody, and meaning.
Karen hymns follow established patterns — predominantly 4/4 meter, verse–chorus form, I–IV–V–I harmony with stepwise melody within an octave. Lyrics use S'gaw Karen script with English chord symbols above the line.

Rooted in community, built for the long term.
The Karen Organization of America exists to support Karen families in the U.S. and to carry the Karen community's voice into national policy regarding Myanmar (Burma).
From the KOA photo archive · Conference gathering
Our mission
Standing with the Karen community in America, here and along the border.
Preserving Karen language across generations, promoting appreciation for cultural identity, and providing a voice for those in Kawthoolei and Burma through advocacy in Washington.
From the KOA photo archive · Outdoor gathering
What we do
KOA's work spans direct support for newly resettled families, civic and cultural programming that keeps Karen identity strong, and policy advocacy that carries Karen voices into U.S. government decisions on Myanmar and Burma.
Education Program
Helping Karen families navigate life in a new country, from early arrival needs to longer-term community connection.
Civic engagement & culture
Karen New Year celebrations, Karen literacy and language resources, and civic programs that pass identity and participation on to the next generation.
Policy advocacy
Engaging Congress and federal agencies on legislation and policy affecting Myanmar and Burma and the Karen people, including the BRAVE Burma Act.
Education training
Guidance for college readiness, academic resources, job applications and interview preparation, citizenship testing, lawful awareness, and English literacy and language resources.
Specific partners, schedules, and referrals are being confirmed.
A voice in the room
“The Karen community's story doesn't end at resettlement — it continues in the decisions Washington makes about Myanmar and Burma.”
— Karen Organization of America
Our approach
We work at three levels at once: direct, practical support for individuals and families; community and cultural programming that keeps identity strong; and policy engagement in Washington, D.C. that connects community experience to legislation and federal decision-making.
Our values
Community first
Programs and advocacy priorities are shaped by what Karen families and community members actually need.
Cultural continuity
Language, tradition, and history are treated as living things worth actively passing on, not just preserving.
Principled advocacy
We engage government processes directly and transparently, on behalf of the community we represent.
Leadership and history
Leadership biographies and confirmed milestones are being prepared for this page.

Five chapters
A story told in song.
၁
The sound of home
Karen hymns carry the memory of villages, refugee camps, and church gatherings across three continents. The music director program preserves these songs in durable digital form.
၂
What we preserve
Handwritten chord charts, church hymnals, oral arrangements, and community recordings — each one catalogued, rights-checked, and archived with provenance.
၃
What we build
A bilingual chord-chart editor with Karen typing mode, print-ready PDF export, and a chord-chart library of 21+ Karen music pieces. Built for Karen music directors in America.
ၔ
Who leads the music
Karen music directors lead worship, teach young singers, and arrange hymns for community gatherings. KOA equips them with tools and connects them to each other.
၅
How to contribute
Upload a handwritten chart, submit a recording, or suggest a lyric correction. Every contribution passes through a rights review before publication.
The editor
A bilingual tool for Karen music directors.
The chord-chart editor supports Karen typing mode, chord placement above lyric lines, and print-ready PDF export. Use it to preserve existing hymns or compose new arrangements.

Karen music style
Meter, melody, and meaning.
Karen hymns follow established patterns — predominantly 4/4 meter, verse–chorus form, I–IV–V–I harmony with stepwise melody within an octave. Lyrics use S'gaw Karen script with English chord symbols above the line.

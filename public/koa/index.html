Karen Organization of America

One people.
One home.

KOA exists so the Karen people in America have one national home — the elders who carried the language across an ocean, the parents keeping it alive at kitchen tables, the kids hearing it for the first time in a new country; the churches and the businesses, the musicians and the athletes, the cooks and the quiet keepers of every old song. One roof for the language, the food, the faith, the games, the news — and the future.

Why this work matters

The organization

Not a monument.
A living thing.

The Karen Organization of America exists so that the S'gaw Karen people in America have a home — a place for the language, the food, the faith, the games, the news, and the future.

  • Language kept alive — and taught, by the people, with new tools no one has built before
  • Community services across the United States, run at the human scale
  • A national registry of Karen churches, businesses, and resources — so you're never alone, ever
  • Music, events, and a story told the way it deserves to be told

What seriousness looks like

A promise should
leave a pattern.

KOA's mission is larger than a launch. These are the standards the work should be judged by as each part is built.

MissionDeclared ToolsIn development Community voiceInvitation open

Elders, families, young people, churches, artists, organizers, and local leaders should shape what KOA makes. Community knowledge is not decoration; it is the starting material.

What exists, what is in development, and what remains an idea should always be named plainly. Trust grows when ambition and evidence are visible side by side.

The work should come back to the community as useful tools, visible milestones, open invitations to participate, and clear ways to correct the course.

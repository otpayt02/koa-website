Karen Organization of America

Rooted in community,
built for the long term.

The Karen Organization of America exists to support Karen families in the U.S. and to carry the Karen community's voice into national policy regarding Myanmar (Burma).

Community first

Programs and advocacy priorities are shaped by what Karen families and community members actually need.

Cultural continuity

Language, tradition, and history are treated as living things worth actively passing on, not just preserving.

Principled advocacy

We engage government processes directly and transparently, on behalf of the community we represent.

What we do

Support that meets people
where they are.

KOA's work spans direct support for newly resettled families, civic and cultural programming that keeps Karen identity strong, and policy advocacy that carries Karen voices into U.S. government decisions on Myanmar/Burma.

Education Program

Helping Karen families navigate life in a new country, from early arrival needs to longer-term community connection. College readiness, academic resources, job applications, interview preparation, citizenship testing, lawful awareness, English literacy.

ResettlementOrientationEnglish

Civic engagement & culture

Karen New Year celebrations, Karen literacy/language classes/resources, and civic programs that pass identity and participation on to the next generation.

Language classesCultural eventsCivic participation

Policy advocacy

Engaging Congress and federal agencies on legislation and policy affecting Myanmar/Burma and the Karen people, including the BRAVE Burma Act.

National Youth Advocacy Program (NYAP)

NYAP connects young Karen Americans with the skills, history, and networks they need to carry the community's advocacy forward — in their own communities and in the halls of government. Leadership training, community history and civics curriculum, direct advocacy experience meeting with policymakers, mentorship and peer network.

Federal advocacy

Carrying Karen voices
into U.S. policy.

KOA engages Congress and federal agencies directly on legislation and policy affecting Myanmar/Burma and the Karen people — and helps community members make their own voices heard. KOA carries concerns directly to Congress and federal agencies — from humanitarian access along the Thai–Myanmar border to legislation like the BRAVE Burma Act.

“The Karen community's story doesn't end at resettlement — it continues in the decisions Washington makes about Myanmar/Burma.” — Karen Organization of America

Current priority: BRAVE Burma Act

The BRAVE Burma Act (H.R. 3190) passed the U.S. House of Representatives on February 9, 2026. KOA has been engaging members of Congress and their staff to build support for the bill and explain what it would mean for the Karen people and broader situation in Myanmar/Burma.
